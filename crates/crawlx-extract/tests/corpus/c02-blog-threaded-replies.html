Planting a Hedge for Birds

A mixed native hedge gives birds shelter in winter, nesting sites in spring and berries in autumn, all in a strip of ground no wider than a path. The trick is to plant several species rather than a single row of one shrub.

Hawthorn and blackthorn make a dense structure that small birds use to hide from predators. Add hazel for nuts, dog rose for hips and a few holly bushes so that there is green cover in the coldest weeks.

Plant the whips in a staggered double row, thirty centimetres apart, and cut them back by half after planting. It feels harsh, but it forces the roots to establish and the hedge to thicken from the base.
