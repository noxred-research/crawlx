Winter Composting Without the Smell

Most gardeners stop thinking about the compost heap when the first frost arrives, and that is exactly when it starts to go wrong. A cold, wet pile slows down, packs together and begins to smell like a drain.

The fix is not more effort but better layering. I keep a sack of shredded cardboard beside the kitchen door and add a generous handful every time the peelings go out. The cardboard soaks up moisture, keeps air moving through the pile and gives the microbes something dry to chew on.

Cover the heap with an old woollen rug or a sheet of thick plastic weighted at the corners. Heat that would otherwise escape stays inside, and the rain stays out.

Turn the pile once every three weeks, even in January. A garden fork pushed in and wiggled around is enough to let fresh air reach the middle. If you find a layer that looks grey and slimy, mix in dry leaves until it crumbles in your hand.

By March the bottom of the heap should be dark and sweet smelling. Sieve it, spread it on the beds and start again.
