Seed Library Lends Heirloom Beans to Gardeners

By Marta Oyelaran, garden correspondent

A card catalog that once held index cards for ship manifests now holds paper envelopes of beans, squash and sunflower seed, and anyone with a library card can borrow them.

How borrowing works

Members choose up to three envelopes at the front desk and sign a short pledge to grow the plants to maturity. In the autumn they return a spoonful of fresh seed, which volunteers dry, label and file back into the drawers.

Most of the stock came from a retired orchard keeper named Ivo Penhallow, who spent forty years saving beans from the terraces above the harbour. His pole bean with purple pods is already the most requested variety.

What comes back

Last year the library recorded sixty returns, but volunteers say the number matters less than the stories attached to them. One borrower returned seed along with a handwritten note about the best way to cook the beans with smoked salt.

The library will hold a free swap evening in the reading hall on the first Friday of April, with tables for labelling, a short talk on drying seed and tea for everyone who comes.
