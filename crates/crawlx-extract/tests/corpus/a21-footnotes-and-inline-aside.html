How the Salt Road Got Its Name

The Salt Road crosses the moor in a long curve that no modern engineer would have drawn. Pack animals followed it from the coastal pans to the market towns inland.1 Each mule carried about two hundred pounds of salt in woven panniers.2

Related reading: Mules and merchants on the moor

The road is mentioned in a charter of the twelfth century, but the name appears much later. A parish clerk wrote it down in a tax roll, probably because it was how local people already spoke of the track.3

Today the path is a popular walk. Stone markers set every mile show how far travellers had to go before the next inn.

Notes

  1. The earliest drawn route is on a parish map kept at the county archive.
  2. The figure comes from a toll ledger of the fourteenth century.
  3. The tax roll was rediscovered during repairs to the vestry roof.
