The Bridge Painters of Lowfield

Every spring a crew of six climbs the iron arches over the Lowfield canal with buckets, brushes and a thermos of tea. They have done the same work for longer than anyone can remember, and the bridge is never quite finished.

Foreman Dagny Holt says the colour is mixed on site from a recipe written in a notebook that goes back four decades. Green is always the base, but each year a little more blue goes in to match the sky above the canal.

The work begins at dawn, before the commuter trains start. By ten the crew is usually on the underside of the bridge, where the old rivets have to be scraped by hand before any paint can go on.

Visitors are welcome to watch from the towpath, and children often leave drawings of the bridge in a box that the crew keeps by the stairs.
